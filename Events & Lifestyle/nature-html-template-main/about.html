Our story

One shovel, three friends, a hillside that had lost everything.

Wildrealm Trust began in 2011 with a borrowed pickup truck, a case of native saplings and a steep, fire-scarred slope that nobody else wanted.

That first planting weekend brought forty neighbours. The second brought eighty. Within three years we had become a registered conservation charity with a small staff, a big nursery and a simple promise: every tree we plant is geotagged and cared for until it can stand alone.

Today our work spans three bioregions — forest, wetland and coast — always in partnership with the people who live closest to the land.

  • Community-first, science-led restoration
  • Every hectare tracked with open data
  • Indigenous knowledge at the heart of our plans

Our values

What the wild taught us

Four principles that have guided every planting, patrol and partnership since day one.

🤝

Community first

Land restores fastest when the people who live on it lead. We hire local, listen first and act together.

🔬

Science led

Native diversity, not quick green. Every site starts with soil, water and species assessments.

🌱

Patience

A forest is a fifty-year project. We measure in decades, and we don't rush the roots.

🪞

Radical transparency

Open budgets, geotagged trees, published audits. Donors see exactly where every dollar lands.

Our journey

Fourteen years of growing wild

The milestones that turned a hillside project into a nationwide movement.

2011Born

The first 1,000 trees

Forty neighbours plant a fire-scarred slope above Mistwater. Every single tree survives the summer.

2015Registered

Wildrealm Trust becomes a charity

A full-time team of five takes over, and the first dedicated native nursery opens its doors.

2019Expanded

Wetlands join the mission

Riverbank restoration begins at Fernwood Creek, bringing beavers — and their dams — back home.

2023Scaled

One millionth tree

A community celebration at Elderpine Reserve marks the millionth sapling planted by Wildrealm hands.

2026Today

34 reserves and counting

1.2M trees in the ground, 2,600 hectares restored and a volunteer family 8,400 members strong.
