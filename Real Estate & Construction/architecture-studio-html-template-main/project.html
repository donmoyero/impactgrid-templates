Nordic Cultural Centre

📍 Copenhagen, Denmark

A 4,200m² cultural venue balancing public openness with intimate gallery spaces. Cross-laminated timber structure with integrated geothermal systems.

The building unfolds as a sequence of thresholds — each space defined by material shift rather than wall. Board-formed concrete at ground level gives way to timber above, reflecting the transition from public to contemplative.

Bremerhof Residential

📍 Berlin, Germany

12-unit housing courtyard with shared gardens and private terraces. Raw concrete frames with oak infill panels and floor-to-ceiling glazing.

The courtyard scheme creates a micro-community — each unit has its own garden, but all share a central green. The concrete frame provides thermal mass; the oak panels bring warmth.

Fjord House

📍 Geiranger, Norway

Private residence cantilevered over a fjord. Weathering steel envelope, triple-height living space, and panoramic glazing oriented toward the water.

The house is a bridge between land and water — its steel structure reaches out over the fjord, framing the view as the primary material. Inside, the triple-height space captures the changing light throughout the day.

Commercial · 2022

Werkstatt Office

📍 Munich, Germany

Adaptive reuse of a 1920s workshop into co-working space. Preserved steel trusses, polished concrete floors, and new mezzanine levels in timber.

The original steel trusses are the hero — we designed around them, not over them. New mezzanine levels in timber create intimate work zones while preserving the industrial volume above.
