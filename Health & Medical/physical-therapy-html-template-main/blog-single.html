7 Daily Stretches for Desk Workers Who Sit Too Much

If you're like most of our patients, you spend seven or eight hours a day in a chair. Hips flexed, shoulders rounded, chin drifting toward the screen. It feels normal — until your body starts sending invoices.

The good news? You don't need a gym, a yoga class or even to leave your desk. A handful of targeted stretches, done for sixty seconds each, can undo most of the damage a sitting day does. Here are the seven we recommend most — the exact ones our therapists give desk workers in clinic.

Before you start

Stretch to a comfortable point of tension, never pain. Breathe steadily and hold each position for 20–30 seconds. If something feels sharp or persists, stop and book an assessment — some "tightness" is actually a problem that needs treatment, not stretching.

  1. Doorway chest opener — stand in a doorway, forearms on the frame, and gently lean forward until you feel a stretch across the chest. Hold 30 seconds.
  2. Seated spinal twist — sit tall, rotate your torso to one side using the chair back for leverage. Hold 25 seconds, switch sides.
  3. Upper trap release — tilt your ear toward your shoulder, then rest your hand on your head for a gentle pull. Hold 20 seconds each side.
  4. Seated hamstring stretch — sit on the edge of your chair, straighten one leg heel-down, and hinge forward. Hold 30 seconds each side.
  5. Figure-four glute stretch — ankle on opposite knee, lean forward over a straight back. Twenty seconds per side unlocks those cranky hips.
  6. Wrist flexor stretch — extend your arm, palm up, and gently press the fingers down with the other hand. Ten seconds each wrist — the keyboard's best friend.
  7. Neck retractions — glide your chin straight back to make a "double chin", hold five seconds, repeat ten times. This one counters forward-head posture all day.
Movement isn't a luxury you earn after the workday — it's the maintenance that keeps your body able to work at all. Ten minutes at your desk is an investment, not an interruption.

Make it a habit

Consistency beats intensity. Attach your stretch break to something you already do — after every meeting, or when your coffee finishes brewing. Set a timer if you need to. In our experience, patients who stretch daily for three weeks rarely go back to the old, achy version of themselves.

And remember: stretching is only half the equation. If you're feeling genuine stiffness or pain that doesn't settle with these moves, come see us. A 60-minute assessment tells you exactly what's tight, what's weak, and what to do about it.
